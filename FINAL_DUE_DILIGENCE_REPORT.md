## CRITICAL COMMERCIAL CONDITION — NO CONVENTIONAL PER-WORKER PLACEMENT FEE

The end-client explicitly rejects the conventional per-worker recruitment/placement fee model.

**What this means:**
- Standard UAE overseas recruitment agencies typically charge AED 1,500–5,000 per worker as a one-time placement fee.
- This model is rejected.

**What is acceptable:**
- Project/batch fee (flat fee for sourcing 100 workers as a single engagement)
- Service retainer + documented cost-recovery (airfare, medical, documentation — no profit margin on workers)
- Management fee for the sourcing and mobilization process
- Any other B2B structure that does not price per individual worker placed

**How to surface this:**
- RFQ Question 7: "Can this be structured WITHOUT a conventional per-worker recruitment/placement fee?"
- RFQ Question 8: "If not, what alternative B2B commercial structure do you propose?"
- This must be in every RFQ. No exceptions.

## WORKER RECRUITMENT FEE RISK — SYSTEMIC WARNING

Worker-paid recruitment fees are a documented systemic problem in all corridors (India, Nepal, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka). These fees are:
- **Illegal under UAE Federal Decree-Law 33/2021, Article 6** (employer/agency cannot charge workers)
- **In violation of ILO Recruitment Principles (2016)**
- **Typically AED 3,500–15,000+ per worker** in the India/Nepal/Bangladesh corridors, paid to home-country sub-agents
- **Not visible to the UAE employer** — paid before the worker arrives

**Required safeguards:**
1. Written zero-fee declaration from the UAE agency (covering both UAE and home-country operations)
2. Payment flow transparency: confirm worker is NOT charged at any stage
3. Source-country specific verification: India (MOIA/MEA RPOA status), Nepal (DOFE licence), Bangladesh (BAIRA/BMET), Philippines (POEA)

## OVERSEAS RECRUITMENT MODEL — VERIFICATION STANDARD

For this requirement, the agency must:
1. Source workers in the home country (not from UAE)
2. Screen and select workers in the home country
3. Handle home-country documentation (exit clearance, emigration, police certificate, medical)
4. Hand over workers to the UAE employer on arrival
5. Workers join the UAE employer's payroll and visa — NOT the agency's

**Disqualifying models:**
- Manpower outsourcing (workers remain on agency's UAE visa)
- On-Demand Labour Supply (ODLS) model (workers on agency's payroll, posted to client)
- UAE-side recruitment only (sourcing workers already in UAE)
